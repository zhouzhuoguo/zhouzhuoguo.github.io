I received my B.S. in Communication Engineering from the School of Electronic and Information Engineering, Beijing Jiaotong University in June 2025. I am currently a first-year Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, advised by Prof. Jun Yao. My current research interests center on bioelectronics and physiological sensing, with an initial focus on mesh-based bioelectronic interfaces. I am also exploring possible connections between AI, signal processing, and bioelectronic systems. You can find more information in my CV.

Selected Projects

AI Accelerator Architecture & Performance Modeling — Duke University (2025)

  • Developed Python-based architecture-level models for AI accelerator workloads, including Point Transformer V2 and diffusion models, to study execution behavior and hardware resource trade-offs.
  • Examined performance-related parameters and power/area assumptions across hardware configurations. Project code.

VLSI Physical Design & Back-End Implementation — Duke University (2026)

Responsible for physical design and back-end implementation of AI accelerator chips, including floorplanning, placement, routing, and implementation verification.

Earlier Academic Projects

🚗 Virtualized Platform-Based 5G Network Planning and Design (Graduation Project)

  • Leveraged the Lingte 5G virtual simulation platform and proposed an end-to-end network-planning framework for cross-regional connected-vehicle scenarios.

📡 Continuous Aperture MIMO for Electromagnetic Information Theory

  • Multi-user XL-MIMO: Uses a random-field model to derive MI expressions under multi-user interference and varying noise, quantifying the effects of discrete sampling and noise power on performance.
  • CAP-MIMO (non-paraxial): Derives tractable MI formulas via dyadic Green’s functions and shows how azimuth, sampling density, and receiver motion govern capacity and MI statistics.
